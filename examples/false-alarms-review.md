# Review: IT Support Analyst (false-alarms example)

How it reads

This is a clear, plain ad with a fair amount going for it. The word counts look alarming, but they come from job vocabulary, not from the way the ad talks to people.

What's working

- A pay range, with "the range is the same for everyone at this level". That answers the negotiation question directly. Evidence: field (Leibbrandt and List, 2015).
- Flexibility shown in use: "most of the team work them".
- Help with childcare costs, named plainly.
- Tasks are concrete and written as tasks.

Three things worth changing

1. Make the flexibility an offer, and move it up. "Feel free to ask about flexible hours" makes the reader do the asking, and it's the last line. Why: flexibility works best when stated, specific and near the top, and people who haven't asked before may not. Evidence: field.
2. Add a line offering adjustments at interview and at work. Why: it raised intention to apply in lab experiments. Evidence: lab; nobody has tested the wording on real applications.
3. Add a short list of essentials, three to five. The ad names tasks but no requirements, so readers can't tell what's essential and may guess high or low. Evidence: guidance (Disability Confident asks for essential requirements only).

Also noticed

- Nothing here carries legal-risk wording.

Who might stop reading

- Someone doing the school run or looking after a parent: probably keeps reading, given the childcare line, and might want the flexible hours stated earlier.
- Someone who'd need an adjustment: no stopping point, but no invitation either. A line would help.
- Someone without a degree: no degree is asked for, so nothing stops them. Say it, if it's true.
- Nobody else obviously stops here.

This is a reading exercise, not research.

Word check

Script-adjusted: 1 communal, 0 agentic (raw 11 communal, 7 agentic). After my read: 0 communal, 0 agentic. Discounted: job and system words ("analyst", "Active Directory", "analytics", "lead generation", "team lead", "support", "Trust", "respond", "responsibilities", "childcare", "competitive pension"), and "agreed" in "agreed times", which is about service levels, not people.

Suggested rewrites

- "Feel free to ask about flexible hours: most of the team work them" → near the top: "Most of the team work flexible hours. Tell us what suits you and we'll sort it."
- (nothing on adjustments) → "Tell us what would help you at interview or at work, and we'll sort it."

Before it goes out

Could someone who'd read it differently from you take a look?
Tools catch words. People catch what the words imply.
