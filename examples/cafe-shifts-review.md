# Review: Café Team Member (cafe-shifts example)

## How it reads

A short, honest ad with the pay in plain sight, but the opening line and the
shift terms may put off more people than the café needs to.

## What's working

- The pay is a real figure (£11.44 an hour), not "competitive". Tips shared
  weekly and free drinks on shift are perks that work for almost anyone.
- "About the role" describes the job in verbs: serve customers, make coffee,
  clear tables, help in the kitchen. A reader knows what the day looks like.
- The physical demands and the transport need are tied to real reasons (the
  deliveries, the missing bus route), so a reader can judge for themselves.
- Plain language throughout.

## Up to three things worth changing

Because the ad is short, who might stop reading is folded into each change.
That part is a reading exercise, not research.

1. **Drop "young, energetic" from the opening line.** Why: it's the first
   thing anyone reads, and it tells someone over 50, or anyone who doesn't
   think of themselves as young, that the job isn't for them before they
   reach the duties. Someone over 50 stops here. They'd keep reading if the
   line described the café and the work instead. "Young" is named by ACAS and
   the EHRC as wording that can be unlawful unless justified; "energetic" is
   commonly flagged. Evidence: field / legal.

2. **Say what the hours actually are.** Why: "Hours vary from week to week"
   and "Must be flexible to work any shifts" ask for flexibility from the
   worker without offering any back. Someone who does the school run or looks
   after a parent stops at "any shifts". They'd keep reading if the ad gave
   typical hours per week, the earliest and latest times, how much notice the
   rota comes with, and whether they can say which shifts they can't do. The
   field evidence is about ads that offer flexibility; this one inverts it.
   Evidence: field.

3. **Word the physical tasks precisely and offer adjustments.** Why: "Able to
   stand for long periods and carry heavy deliveries" names real tasks, which
   is good, but "long" and "heavy" are open-ended, and there's no offer of
   adjustments. Someone who'd need an adjustment at interview stops here.
   They'd keep reading if the line gave the actual weight and how deliveries
   are shared, and said what help is available. Evidence: lab / legal.

## Also noticed

- **Pay rate (legal, outside this skill's evidence base).** If this job is in
  the UK, check £11.44 against the current National Minimum Wage. That was the
  rate for workers aged 21 and over in 2024 to 2025, and it has risen since.
  Next to "young" in the opening line, a low rate may also read as the café
  wanting someone on a youth rate. The ad doesn't say whether the rate is
  fixed or rises with experience. Someone who has never negotiated pay won't
  ask.
- **Seven must-haves for an entry-level job.** Barista experience and a food
  hygiene certificate are things many cafés train for. If the café would
  train the right person, move them to "nice to have" and say so. Someone
  without café experience stops at "Barista experience". Evidence: field (for
  volume of applicants, not for who applies).
- **"A bubbly personality".** This is a personality, not a behaviour, and it
  isn't on the word lists. In my view it reads as young and often as
  feminine, and may put off quieter people who are good with customers.
  Describing what friendliness looks like in the job would let a candidate
  show it. Evidence: untested.
- **"Own transport".** The reason is given, which helps. A disabled person or
  anyone who can't drive may still stop here. If a cycle route, a lift share
  or a pick-up from the nearest stop is possible, saying so would keep them
  reading. Evidence: untested.
- **No line on adjustments or how to apply.** The ad ends at the perks list.
  Adding how to apply, and that adjustments are available, gives every reader
  a next step. Evidence: lab / guidance.

## Word check

Script-adjusted: 1 communal, 0 agentic. After my read: 0 communal, 0 agentic.
Discounted: "shared" in "Tips shared weekly" describes how tips are paid, not
a trait. "Young", "energetic", "team player" and
"bubbly" aren't on the lists and are covered in the findings above.

## Suggested rewrites

- "The Old Mill Café is looking for a young, energetic team player to join our
  busy café in the heart of town." → "The Old Mill Café is a busy café in the
  heart of town. We're looking for someone to join the team serving customers
  and keeping things running through the rush."
- "Hours vary from week to week." and "Must be flexible to work any shifts,
  including early mornings, evenings and bank holidays" → "Shifts are between
  `[earliest]` and `[latest]`, about `[N]` hours a week. You'll see the rota
  `[N]` days ahead, and you can tell us which shifts you can't do. Weekends and
  bank holidays are part of the job, about `[how often]`."
- "Able to stand for long periods and carry heavy deliveries" → "You'll spend
  most of a shift on your feet and help unpack deliveries of up to `[weight]`.
  Tell us what would help you at interview or at work, and we'll sort it."
- "Barista experience" and "Food hygiene certificate" → "Nice to have: barista
  experience and a food hygiene certificate. `[We'll train you and pay for the
  certificate.]`"
- "A bubbly personality" → "You enjoy chatting to customers, you notice when
  someone's been waiting, and you keep things friendly when it's busy."
