# Review: Customer Success Manager ("before" version)

How it reads

This is a solid, plain ad that already shows its salary, with a few things that may quietly make some readers think twice.

What's working

- It gives a pay range up front, which many ads don't.
- Clear headings, concrete detail ("a portfolio of 150 accounts") and tasks named as tasks.
- Holiday and pension are stated, not just implied.
- The company says what it does, in everyday words.

Three things worth changing

1. Fix the two pay figures. The top says £32k to £38k and the offer says "an attractive salary of £35k to £42k". Pick one range, state it once near the top, and say whether it's negotiable. Why: a reader can't tell which is real, and unclear terms favour whoever feels entitled to ask. Evidence: field (Leibbrandt and List, 2015).
2. Move flexibility up and make it specific. "Flexible working" is one line near the bottom of the perks list. Why: ads offering flexibility drew roughly 20 to 30% more applicants in a UK trial, likely an overestimate, and it works best when stated, specific and near the top. Evidence: field.
3. Trim the requirements to the real essentials. Twelve must-haves includes a degree and a full UK driving licence. Why: fewer requirements raised applications by about 7% in one field study of real ads, though not the share of women. A licence for a job that doesn't involve driving can read as a physical proxy, and the degree may stand in for skills. Evidence: field for volume; lab for the disability point; untested for the degree, my view.

Also noticed

- "Confident, ambitious" and "the decisive voice of the customer" describe the person, not the work. Evidence: contested (field studies disagree on how much this changes who applies).
- No offer of adjustments at interview or at work. Evidence: lab (Pike and colleagues, 2026); not tested on real applications.
- "Team socials every month" is fine, but if they centre on drinks, a mix would suit more people. Evidence: guidance, and untested in ads.

Who might stop reading

- Someone doing the school run or caring for a parent: stops at "Flexible working", the fourth bullet of five. Would keep reading if it said which hours or days are fixed.
- Someone who'd need an adjustment: stops at "Full UK driving licence". Would keep reading if the licence went, or the ad said why it's needed, plus an adjustments line.
- Someone who has never negotiated pay: stops at the two ranges. Would keep reading if there was one range and "negotiable within it".
- Someone without a degree: stops at "A degree in business or a related subject". Would keep reading if it named the skill the degree stood for.
- Someone over 50: nothing obviously stops them here.

This is a reading exercise, not research.

Word check

Script-adjusted: 0 communal, 7 agentic (raw 2 communal, 10 agentic). After my read: 0 communal, 3 agentic worth changing ("confident, ambitious", "decisive"). Discounted: "analytical skills" and "confident presenting" (tied to the job), "champion our customers" (it's the role), "We're ambitious" (about the company), and fixed terms ("independent cafés", "decisions", "competing priorities", "depending", "Trustpilot").

Suggested rewrites

- "£32k to £38k" and "an attractive salary of £35k to £42k" → one line near the top: "[£X to £Y], depending on experience. Pay is negotiable within the range."
- "Flexible working" → near the top: "Core hours are [10 to 3]. Fit the rest around your life."
- "A confident, ambitious Customer Success Manager" → "A Customer Success Manager ready to take on a portfolio of 150 accounts and grow into [the next step]"
- "A degree in business or a related subject" → "[the skill the degree stood for]", or move it to "Nice to have"
- "Full UK driving licence" → remove it, or say why the job needs one
