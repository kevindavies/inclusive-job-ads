# Review: Part-time Bookkeeper (good example)

## How it reads

This is a warm, clear ad that tells most readers they'd fit before they reach the second paragraph.

## What's working

- Pay comes first, as a range for the actual hours, and it says the top is negotiable. That's the line with the strongest field evidence behind it: saying pay is negotiable closed the gap in who asked for more (Leibbrandt and List, 2015).
- Flexibility is near the top and specific: you choose the days, the work mostly fits school hours, two days can be from home. Flexibility stated this plainly has field evidence for drawing more applicants.
- Three essentials, each a thing you've done rather than a number of years. Qualifications sit under "Nice to have", and there's no degree.
- No age wording, no gendered terms, no physical requirements.
- "Working here" shows rather than claims. The story about the owners on the shop floor with a cash tin says more than any values statement could.
- The adjustments offer is there, in plain words, and "just a few lines about what you've done" lowers the bar for anyone without a polished CV.

## Up to three things worth changing

1. **Say whether the bookkeeper is expected on the shop floor too.** Why: the Christmas story is the ad's best line, but it leaves a question. Someone who can't stand at a till for hours, or who chose this job because it's a desk job, may wonder if "nobody thinks a job is beneath them" means they'll be covering the tills in December. One sentence answers it either way. Evidence: untested (my view); it touches the disability check, where unstated physical expectations lowered intention to apply in lab studies.
2. **Say where the garden centre is, and which day is on site.** Why: "work from home on two of them" implies one day on site, but there's no town. Someone working around school hours or a parent's care will want to know the journey before they apply. Evidence: untested (my view); it builds on flexibility, which has field evidence.
3. **Say where to apply and what the interview involves.** Why: the ad ends without an address or a closing date. The adjustments offer is good, and it's easier to use when people know what they're asking adjustments for: a chat, a test on Xero, a visit. Evidence: guidance (Disability Confident asks for adjustments to be offered early); the interview detail itself is untested.

Who might stop reading, folded in: a parent on the school run keeps reading at line 6. Someone over 50 or without a degree finds nothing to stop them. Someone who'd need an adjustment, or can't do physical work, might pause at the Christmas story (change 1). Someone who has never negotiated pay is told the top is negotiable, which is the right signal. This is a reading exercise, not research.

## Also noticed

- No legal-risk wording found: no gendered titles or pronouns, no age wording, no physical requirements.
- "You choose which three days" sits alongside a weekly payroll. If payroll has to run on a set day, say so, so nobody picks days that don't work.

## Word check

Script-adjusted: 0 communal, 0 agentic. Raw: 0 communal, 0 agentic. After my read: 0 and 0; nothing to discount. The ad describes the job in verbs ("keep the books", "explain a set of numbers") rather than traits, so the lists have nothing to catch.

## Suggested rewrites

- "When the tills went down last Christmas, the owners were on the shop floor with a cash tin. Nobody here thinks a job is beneath them." → "When the tills went down last Christmas, the owners were on the shop floor with a cash tin. Nobody here thinks a job is beneath them. [You won't be asked to work the tills. / At Christmas we all help out for a few hours; if that won't work for you, tell us.]"
- "You choose which three days. Most of the work fits school hours, and you can work from home on two of them." → "You choose which three days. Most of the work fits school hours. One day a week is at the garden centre in [town]; the other two can be from home."
- "Send a CV, or just a few lines about what you've done." → "Send a CV, or just a few lines about what you've done, to [email] by [closing date]. The interview is [what it involves, and how long]."
